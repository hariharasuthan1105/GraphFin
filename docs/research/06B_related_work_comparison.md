## Exact comparison

| Prior work | Established contribution relevant here | GraphFin implementation and distinction | What GraphFin has not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 — closest predecessor | Transaction-graph features, reduced egonets, circular/random-walk features and unsupervised anomaly detection including Isolation Forest. | `feature_service.py` and E1–E5 use graph summaries and IF. E5 uses only four egonet/circular columns; its one-pass reduction and unweighted two/three-hop closure heuristic differ from the published construction. | Graph features plus IF is not new. E5 is **not a faithful full reproduction of L1**; neither a superiority claim nor a paper-level baseline claim follows. |
| L2 | Weighted-egonet structural regularities and anomalous departures. | GraphFin turns graph/behavior/time summaries into account vectors. It does not fit OddBall's structural relationships. | No comparison with OddBall, and no novelty in merely using egonet statistics. |
| L3 | Finding laundering-related flow structures across multipartite graphs. | GraphFin ranks individual accounts from fixed summaries rather than optimizing a flow-chain objective. | Node-level endpoint-involvement AP is not directly comparable to flow-subgraph discovery. |
| L4 | Coupled-tensor flow analysis incorporating multiple transaction attributes. | GraphFin collapses history into account statistics rather than coupled tensors. | No equivalent tensor baseline or common-task experiment. |
| L5 | Stream-based laundering-agent detection using transaction/balance behavior. | GraphFin has retrospective rate and inter-event-gap features; the current split is by account on a full-observation graph. | Temporal features do not demonstrate streaming, prospective detection or future-time generalization. |
| L6 | Minimal graph changes that alter a GNN prediction. | GraphFin omits identified historical transactions under count/money constraints and recomputes features for a frozen IF. | No GNN, minimality proof, actionable recourse or required prediction flip. Call these evidence interventions, not proven counterfactual causes. |
| L7 | Removal-based explanation is an established family with different removal/response/aggregation choices. | GraphFin removes events rather than independently masking feature columns, preserving the graph-to-feature dependency path. | Score response alone does not establish explanation correctness or user utility. |
| L8 | Broad, controlled comparison of anomaly detectors across datasets. | GraphFin saves splits, versions, scores, hashes and negative findings, but its main evidence is two supplied IBM subsets. | Two related subsets are not broad cross-domain validation. ADBench is not itself proof of GraphFin source-to-target transfer. |
| L9 | Evaluation must distinguish training information, model selection, metrics and dataset conditions. | GraphFin freezes fitting/selection rules and prohibits target-label tuning; historical transfer results remain quarantined by provenance. | Unsupervised fitting, one-class training and supervised model selection are not interchangeable. A working transfer API does not establish transfer accuracy. |
| L10 | A synthetic AML generator and datasets, with known simulated outcomes. | GraphFin uses supplied transformed IBM files and endpoint-involvement labels. | The publication does not authenticate these particular transformed bytes or recover their missing raw conversion lineage. Synthetic results do not establish bank deployment validity. |

## Novelty judgment

The strongest defensible contribution is **an auditable, versioned empirical system and a negative-results study**: correction of split/model and tied-score evaluation errors, source-only fitting with explicit transductive scope, same-population baseline comparisons, and transaction-level evidence diagnostics that retain unavailability and computation cost. These are useful project-specific engineering and validation contributions, not a newly established general ML methodology.

Candidate general claims about evidence specificity, better explanations, scalable graph sensitivity or robustness-aware ranking require evidence that does not currently exist. Phase 5H stopped ranking development; Phase 6A's twelve compatible explanation comparisons all tied. Literature overlap is substantial, particularly L1 and L7. A focused applied/empirical paper or system demonstration is a more plausible direction than a methodology-oriented ML paper, conditional on the validation and provenance work in `06B_manuscript_revision_plan.md`.
